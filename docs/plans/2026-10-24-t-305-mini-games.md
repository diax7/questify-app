## The split (ADR-214)

The task is three pieces of different risk; one worker cannot do all of it well, and one piece is blocked.

- **T-305 (this plan)**: the round machinery for all four games (issue, replay, verdict, attempts, pay, flags, sweep,
  retry, switch, builder), the shared replay engine interface, and **memory match** and **slide puzzle** end to end
  (engines, app screens, default art). Both are turn-based: an input is a discrete tap, so the replay is exact.
- **T-305c (new)**: **catch** and **tap rush**, the two real-time games, on T-305's machinery: fixed-tick simulation,
  position samples, reaction-time bounds. Core too (their engines are the replay). Their rules are sketched below so
  this review covers the protocol for all four; the builder refuses `catch` and `tap_rush` with "not available yet"
  until T-305c (the `photo` pattern of ADR-183).
- **T-305b (new, blocked on Q-149)**: organizer images on cards, puzzle pictures and falling items (FR-1808). Images
  need an upload and a bucket, and CranL Pro's one bucket is `b7r-media` (ADR-183, Q-149). Until then every game uses
  Questify's default art. FR-1808's second case ("no image: the default pictures show") is what T-305 ships.

## What a round proves (ADR-212)

The server, not the phone, decides every result (FR-1805):

1. **Issue.** The player taps Play. The database makes a round: a 32-bit seed from `gen_random_uuid()` (no extension
   needed), the server's `issued_at`, the game and level frozen from the quest, the level's numbers frozen as
   `params`, the quest's points frozen, and a `deadline_at = issued_at + params.timeLimit + 10 minutes` (FR-1809's
   window). Issuing **uses an attempt** (FR-1807): a round abandoned, crashed or never sent counts as played, so a
   player cannot fish for an easy layout by quitting.
2. **Play.** The app builds the board from `(game, seed, params)` with the shared engine and records the player's
   inputs with times in milliseconds from the moment the board showed.
3. **Result.** The app sends `{inputs, claimed}` (`claimed` is the engine's own result on the phone). The API replays
   the inputs with the **same shared engine** from the frozen round, never reading a score from the phone, then hands
   the database the verdict. The database checks the clock and the window itself and pays.

**Verdicts:**

- `passed`: the replay reaches the level's target. Pays once, on the first pass (FR-1806).
- `short`: legal play that falls short. No points; the answer shows the target and the result (FR-217's second case).
- `refused` (**impossible**, FR-218, FR-1805's second case): any of an illegal input (a card that is not there, a flip
  during the mismatch pause, a tile not next to the gap), inputs out of order or past the time limit, two inputs closer
  than the human floor (100 ms), more inputs than the game's cap, a `claimed` result the replay does not reproduce, or
  inputs that span more time than the server saw pass (`last input > (now − issued_at) + 3 s`: nobody plays 60 s in
  30 s of wall time). The round counts as played, nothing is paid, and a `minigame` flag is raised or extended.
- `passed` **and flagged** (**unlikely**): legal and passing, but beyond what a person usually does (memory: too many
  lucky first-sight matches; slide: solved in fewer moves than the shuffle with a very fast average gap). Paid, because
  a person can be that lucky, and flagged for the organizer (flags never block play, FR-329, ADR-153).

**An honest player is never refused** (CTO 1). Two rules make every impossible case unreachable from the real app:

- **The engine version is frozen on the round.** Each game in the shared registry has an `engineVersion` (an integer,
  bumped whenever a change alters what a replay gives). The app sends the versions it carries at issue
  (`engineVersions: {memory_match: 1, …}`); when its version for the quest's game is below the server's, issue answers
  `426 update_required` ("Update the app to play this game") **before** an attempt is used. The round keeps
  `engine_version`, and the API replays it with that version's engine: an older engine stays in
  `packages/shared/src/games/<game>/v<n>.ts` until no open round uses it (at most the longest window after a deploy),
  then is deleted.
- **The board asks the engine before it records a tap.** The engine exposes `canInput(state, input)`, the same rules
  `replay` enforces (the 100 ms floor, the mismatch pause, a tile next to the gap, a card still face down, the cap, the
  time limit). The app's board drops any tap it refuses, as it already ignores flips during the pause. Times come from
  a monotonic clock (`performance.now()`) started when the board shows, after the round arrived, so `last input` is
  always below the server's elapsed time; the 3 s slack covers a phone clock running fast. A test drives the app's input
  gate with random tap streams over many seeds and asserts the replay is never `impossible`.

**What it cannot prove** (stated in ADR-212): the seed reaches the phone, so a modified app can see the whole layout
(memory pairs, the puzzle's shuffle) and play it perfectly at human speed. The targets are set below perfect play, the
"unlikely" checks catch the cheap version of that, and the reward is one quest's points once. Hiding the layout would
mean a round trip per flip on a venue network (the quiz's model), which the games' feel cannot afford. A player who
studies the seed for nine minutes and then sends 40 s of inputs passes the clock check; the window bounds it.

## The games in this task (ADR-215; numbers are tuning, Q-178)

The level table lives in shared code (`packages/shared/src/games/levels.ts`) and is **sent with every round**: the app
renders from the round's `params`, so retuning is a server deploy, never a store release. The organizer picks only
easy, medium or hard (FR-1806).

| Game | Level | Board | Time limit | Pass target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Memory match | easy | 6 pairs (3×4) | 120 s | all pairs found |
| | medium | 8 pairs (4×4) | 120 s | all pairs within 20 moves |
| | hard | 10 pairs (4×5) | 120 s | all pairs within 22 moves |
| Slide puzzle | easy | 3×3, shuffled by a 20-step walk | 180 s | solved |
| | medium | 3×3, 60-step walk | 180 s | solved |
| | hard | 4×4, 120-step walk | 300 s | solved |

- **Memory match.** The seed shuffles the pairs (Fisher–Yates on the shared PRNG). An input is `{t, card}`. A move is
  two flips. A matched pair stays up; a mismatch shows both for 700 ms, during which the app takes no flips (a flip in
  that pause is impossible: the real app cannot send it). Result: `{pairs, moves, ms, lucky}`. Lucky: the second flip
  of a move is a card never seen before and matches, while at least 4 unseen cards remained; **unlikely** at 3 or
  more. Cap: 400 flips.
- **Slide puzzle.** The seed drives a random walk from the solved board (never undoing the last step; always
  solvable; re-walked if it lands solved). An input is `{t, tile}`: the tapped tile must be next to the gap (one tile
  per tap; no row slides in v1). Result: `{solved, moves, ms}`. **Unlikely**: solved in fewer moves than the walk and
  an average gap under 300 ms. Cap: 1,000 moves. The picture is one of three default illustrations, chosen by the seed.

**The engine contract** (`packages/shared/src/games/`): `prng.ts` (a 32-bit generator with `Math.imul` and `>>> 0`
only, golden vectors tested, so Node and release Hermes produce the same sequence: no floats, no `Math.random`, no
`Math.sin`; the database's seed is a signed `integer`, so the generator starts from `seed >>> 0`, with golden vectors
for 0 and a negative seed, CTO 4), `engine.ts` (`GameEngine<Input, Result>`: `engineVersion`, `parseInputs` (zod:
shape and caps), `start(seed, params)`, `canInput(state, input)`, `replay(seed, params, inputs) → {result} |
{impossible: reason}`, `passes(result, params)`, `unlikely(result, params)`), one folder per game,
and a registry keyed by game. The same functions drive the app's board, so an honest app's `claimed` always matches.
Integer arithmetic only; no built-in missing in release Hermes (`hermes-runtime.test.ts`).

**T-305c's games, for the protocol only** (built there): both run a fixed 30 Hz tick from the seed. Catch: the input
is `{tick, x}` targets for the basket, which the simulation moves at a capped speed (so impossible movement cannot be
expressed); unlikely when the basket heads to an item's landing spot before the item appears, too often. Tap rush: the
input is `{t, x, y}`; a hit sooner than 120 ms after a target appears is beyond human reaction, and more than a fifth
of hits like that is impossible. Their inputs fit the same `{inputs, claimed}` body and the same verdicts. Catch
records a basket target **only when it changes** (not every tick), so a 60 s round stays far under the 64 KB body
limit; if a game cannot, the body limit becomes per game in T-305c (CTO 9).

## Attempts, the pass and the retry (ADR-213)

- **One quest type `game`**, `type_config: {game, level, attempts, cooldownMinutes}`: `game` in `memory_match`,
  `slide_puzzle` (T-305c adds `catch`, `tap_rush`); `level` in `easy`, `medium`, `hard`; `attempts` an integer 1 to
  10 (default 1) or `null` for unlimited; `cooldownMinutes` 1 to 1,440, required with unlimited and refused otherwise
  (F-309: "one, a few, or unlimited with a cooldown"). `repeat_cap = 1` (a check, as `quest_vote_quiz_once`). Not a
  refreshing type (`quest_refresh_type`'s list already refuses it). Verification list empty: `complete_quest` and the
  operator's My QR scan answer `wrong_method`.
- **The config is locked once a round exists** (CTO 3): raising attempts would re-open a quest already counted done
  (and re-lock an in-order gate after its bonus paid), lowering them would leave a negative count, and a new game or
  level would break the player's view. `quest_structure` (latest 0042:67) gains a `game` case (the whole
  `type_config`) and `quest_lock_played` (latest 0042:91) is made again with `game_round` beside the vote, quiz and
  survey rows, its trigger re-created for `OLD.type IN ('vote', 'quiz', 'survey', 'game')`: `409 quest_locked` ("players
  have played this quest: only its text may change"), as for a quiz. Points stay editable (each round froze its own).
- **Attempts are counted at issue**: rounds issued for the quest by the player. With attempts left and no pass, Play
  issues a new round; a still-open round of the same quest is ended `abandoned` (at most one open per player and
  quest). Unlimited: a new round only `cooldownMinutes` after the previous round's issue (`409 cooldown {retryAt}`).
- **Issue is idempotent** (Idempotency-Key required): a network retry of the same Play returns the same open round and
  never uses a second attempt. The key is stored on the round (`UNIQUE (enrollment_id, issue_key)`).
- **Pays once on the first pass** (FR-1806): `pay_completion(enrolled, quest, round.points, 'game', 'game:' || round.id,
  NULL, NULL, round.device)` — the item on a first completion and the category bonus follow, as for every payer. After
  a pass, Play reads "Passed" and issuing answers `409 already_completed`. A second passing result of the same round
  (another key) answers `finished`; the same key answers the first result (`replayed`).
- **All attempts used without a pass counts the quest done** (like a quiz with no right answers, 0044): `quest_done`
  (latest 0046) is made again with a third clause, so an in-order category never dead-ends behind a game nobody can
  pass. When the last attempt ends without a pass (a `short` or `refused` result, or the sweep closing it), the payer
  calls `pay_category_bonus(enrolled, category, NULL)` as `finish_quiz` does. Unlimited attempts never reach this.
- **The result window** (FR-1809): a result is taken until `deadline_at` (time limit + 10 minutes from issue). The app
  keeps the finished round's inputs **in memory** and offers Retry; after the window, Retry answers `409 expired`:
  "This round counted as played". Memory only (no new storage dependency; SecureStore's size limit does not fit a
  round's inputs): an app killed before the retry loses the round, which counts as played (Q-177). While the app holds
  an unsent result, the quest sheet shows **Retry instead of Play** (CTO 6), so a new round never silently ends the one
  waiting to be sent; Play returns once the result is sent or its window ends.
- **The sweep**: the lifecycle tick (`events/tick.ts`, after the quiz sweep) calls `close_one_due_round()` in a loop
  with the quiz sweep's shape: rounds `open` past `deadline_at` become `expired`; when that was the last attempt, the
  bonus call above. Reads never write: until the sweep runs, a read treats an open round past its deadline as
  expired.
- **Leaving the event** (ban, removal, leave, deletion): an `AFTER UPDATE OF status ON enrollment` trigger ends the
  player's open rounds `abandoned` (the `void_open_quizzes` pattern).
- **Manual award** (T-207) of a game quest pays as for any quest and makes it done; an open round's later pass answers
  `done` and pays nothing. `remove_award` reverses the points; the round keeps its verdict (ADR-152's rule).
- **Event switch** `miniGames` (`config.features.miniGames`, default on; FR-1009), the meet pattern: the platform's
  switch checked by the API, the event's by the database (`games_on(event)`). Off: game quests hidden
  (`src/quests/switched-off.ts`), issue answers `403 feature_off`. A round issued before the switch went off may still
  send its result inside its window: the player was mid-game. Allowed in every event kind (a virtual event plays games
  too).
- **Older builds** do not know `game`: they show the quest with their "update the app" line and no action.

  which the API maps (`refused` → 422 after the commit).

## Locks (ADR-211's rule holds)

A game payer locks **one** enrollment, so ADR-211's multi-enrollment rule is met trivially. Every writer of
`game_round` takes the same order: `event_rehearsal FOR SHARE` (`ensure_rehearsal_wiped`), [the event `FOR KEY SHARE`
in the sweep only, as the quiz sweep], the enrollment `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`, the round `FOR UPDATE`, then
`pay_completion`'s order (items in id order, redemptions, the one device advisory key in the completion trigger, the
player's XP row), then flag rows. Flag rows after the enrollment is the order `raise_completion_flags` already uses; an
owner's ban from the Flags page that crosses it is aborted by Postgres (40P01) and answered `503 busy` with
Retry-After (`conventions.ts`), and the app retries the result with the same key (safe: the replay answers it). The
API holds **no** transaction while it replays: it reads the frozen round, replays in memory, then opens the one
transaction for `finish_game_round`.

A DB test runs, on two connections with a barrier, 50 rounds each: the same result twice at once (one verdict, the
second `replayed`); two results of one round under two keys (one `passed`, the other `finished`); a result against
the sweep closing the same round at its deadline; a result against the player's ban; a pass beside a QR scan on the
same phone; a pass beside a manual award of the same quest (one payment). No deadlock error escapes as anything but
`busy`.

## App

- **The quest sheet**: game name and picture, the level ("Medium"), the target in words ("Find all 8 pairs in 20 moves
  or fewer"), the points, the attempts ("2 of 3 left", "Next try at 14:05" with unlimited), and **Play**; "Passed" after
  a pass; "Already played" with one attempt used (FR-1807's first case); "No attempts left" (second case).

## Proposed decisions and questions

- **ADR-212** Mini-game rounds and replay: the database issues each round (a seed from `gen_random_uuid`, its own
  clock, the level frozen from its own copy of the level table); the API replays the phone's inputs with the shared
  engine of the version frozen on the round and never trusts a reported score; an app with an older engine is told to
  update before an attempt is used, and the app's board refuses every tap the replay would call impossible, so an
  honest player is never refused; impossible inputs (illegal, too fast, out of order, past the limit, over
  the cap, a claimed result the replay does not reproduce, more play time than the server saw) refuse the round, which
  counts as played, and raise a `minigame` flag; legal but unlikely play is paid and flagged; the refusal's reason is
  shown to the organizer only. The seed reaching the phone is accepted: targets sit below perfect play. Q-176.
- **ADR-213** Mini-game attempts, pass and retry: one quest type `game` with a game and a level; attempts 1 to 10
  (default 1) or unlimited with a cooldown of 1 to 1,440 minutes, used at issue; Play is idempotent; pays the frozen
  points once, on the first pass; all attempts used without a pass counts the quest done (gates and category bonus);
  the result window is the time limit plus 10 minutes and the app keeps the inputs in memory only; the event switch
  `miniGames` is on by default and an issued round may still finish after it goes off; allowed in every event kind;
  the game's config is locked once a round exists (points stay editable); Play is hidden while a result waits for
  Retry. Q-177.
- **ADR-214** T-305 split: T-305 builds the round machinery and memory match and slide puzzle; T-305c builds catch and
  tap rush on it; T-305b builds organizer images once a Questify bucket exists (Q-149); until then the default art
  shows. Q-179.
- **ADR-215** Mini-game levels are Questify tuning kept on the server and sent with each round (retuning is a deploy,
  not a store release); the organizer picks easy, medium or hard; the board never mirrors in Arabic; motion uses React
  Native's own `Animated`, so the games add no native module. Q-178.
- **Q-176** Agree that a modified app could see the layout (the seed) and that the targets, the unlikely flags and a
  one-time reward bound it, rather than a server round trip per move?
- **Q-177** Agree: attempts used when Play is tapped; the result kept in memory only (a closed app loses it); an
  issued round may finish after the switch goes off?
- **Q-178** The level numbers (table above) and the unlikely thresholds (3 lucky matches; fewer moves than the shuffle
  at under 300 ms a move): keep as a start, to be tuned after a playtest?
- **Q-179** Agree to the split, and that organizer images wait for the Questify bucket (Q-149)?
